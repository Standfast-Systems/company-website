Case study

More output, less new code

Work done as a Staff Software Engineer at Total Quality Logistics, not a Standfast Systems engagement. AI-assisted delivery on a behind-schedule enterprise modernization, directed by judgment and measured sprint by sprint.

Role
Staff Software Engineer, Total Quality Logistics
Program
Dispatch modernization, five teams, two shared codebases
Measured
13 two-week sprints, Azure DevOps and GitHub
Work type
Employee work, not a Standfast Systems build

The situation

A behind-schedule, multi-year modernization of the Dispatch system at the core of TQL's freight brokerage. Five teams delivered vertical slices across two shared codebases. The team was engineers new to modernization plus a group of near-shore contractors.

What we did

The other staff engineers and I built an AI code-review assistant into our infrastructure, wired into Azure DevOps tickets, the engineering handbook and project-level details. I led the weekly sync with the other team leads that kept it aimed at what the modernization actually needed. It let the senior reviewers focus on the changes that mattered, and it widened what counted as boilerplate enough that we could go faster without just making a bigger mess.

The result

Over roughly six months, sustained sprint velocity rose about 1.5× and held, peaking at more than double where it started. Defect rates stayed flat. The program reached development-complete on schedule while absorbing another team's scope.

What the data shows

Code volume spiked early, while AI capacity went into refactoring and paying down inherited technical debt. Then it fell, while delivered velocity kept climbing. More output on less new code only happens when AI capacity is directed by judgment.
